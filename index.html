Issue #92 · Milestone: Shared components

Promptomat Logo System

The chosen mark, specified. A capital P knocked out of a rounded gold tile — one stroke weight, round terminals, all curvature from a single circle. This board fixes the geometry, the lockups, the size floor and the colour behaviour, then shows the mark doing its actual jobs: browser tab, navigation bar, and the sign-in and sign-up screens.

Gold #e1ad66 Tile 120×120, r30 Stroke 24u · round Ink 82% of tile

01

The mark

The P is not filled artwork — it is two stroked paths used as a luminance mask, so the counter is a genuine hole. Whatever sits behind the tile shows through it. That is the property the whole system rests on, and it is why the mark cannot be flattened to a single filled path on export.

Grid · 12u module
Clear space = corner radius
Glyph alone · 7:8
Tile
120×120 with a 30u corner radius, filled #e1ad66. The radius is exactly a quarter of the side, so the corner reads as a squircle rather than a rounded square.
Ink
The two paths are drawn in a 120×120 local space and placed by translate(10.8,10.8) scale(0.82) translate(6,0). The 0.82 factor is the “ink sits at 82% of the tile” rule; the trailing translate(6,0) optically centres a letter whose mass is left-heavy.
Stroke
24u, round cap, round join — one weight everywhere. Stem, bowl and every terminal are the same width, which is what keeps the mark stable when it is scaled down.
Counter
The bowl is a half-circle of radius 24u centred at (60, 48). Its inner edge therefore sits 12u from that centre, and the bowl ends at (60, 72) without returning to the stem — the counter is open at the bottom left, not sealed.
Glyph
Cropped on its own, the ink fills viewBox="12 12 84 96" exactly — an 84×96 box, 7:8. Use this form wherever the mark has to take a single colour.

The wordmark face is settled

The lockups above are set in Archivo Bold, which is what the app renders. Archivo is already self-hosted in the bundle as the base family, so the wordmark costs nothing extra to ship and cannot drift from the interface it sits in. Earlier boards tried Silkscreen and a serif; both were dropped for the same reason — they would have added a face the product does not otherwise load. One requirement survives regardless: the wordmark has to be converted to outlines when the lockup is exported, so the asset never depends on a font being available.

16px, magnified 6×

At a 16px render the counter is about 2.6px across and the stem-to-bowl gap about 2.6px. Both survive on a 2× display and both close up on a 1× one. Ship the favicon as SVG — browsers rasterise it at 32px for the tab and 48px for bookmarks, which clears the floor. Do not generate a 16×16 ICO from this file without redrawing the counter.

04

Colour

The tile carries the colour and the P carries none — it is a hole. So the mark inherits its ground automatically, and there is no “on dark” and “on light” variant to maintain. Contrast figures below are the tile against the ground behind it.

Canvas 9.61 : 1
Card 8.86 : 1
Raised 8.24 : 1
Light ground 2.02 : 1 — fails
One colour 17.68 : 1
Inverted 9.61 : 1

On a light ground the tile fails. Gold on #f3f4f6 is 2.02:1, well under the 3:1 that a meaningful graphic needs. There is no light theme in the product today, but the mark will land on light grounds the moment it goes on a slide, a README or an OG image. The fix is the mark in a single dark ink — the tile filled #0a0d12, or the bare glyph from 01 where a tile would sit too heavy — not a lighter gold. Either variant has to be exported alongside the tile rather than improvised later.

#e1ad66 — the logo The gold in the mark as delivered. Accent-400 in the concept doc’s ramp.
#c89b5e — --text-accent The accent already shipped in tokens.css, and the gold board 1 was drawn in.

These are two different golds and only one can win. They sit 1.25:1 apart — close enough to look like a rendering error rather than a decision when they appear on the same screen, which they will: the nav mark against a gold focus ring, or the logo above a link tinted --text-accent. Either retune --text-accent and --border-accent to #e1ad66, or redraw the tile at #c89b5e. Not a rendering detail to settle in review — it is a token change either way.

The tile is already a squircle, so it must not be masked into another one. iOS, Android and the macOS dock all apply their own corner shape to an app icon. Feed them the tile as-is and the radius is applied twice, which visibly thins the corners. For those targets export the glyph on a full-bleed gold square with no radius of its own and let the platform cut the corner.

The auth screen has exactly one warm thing on it, and it is the logo. --surface-accent is #e6e9ee, so the primary button is near-white, not gold. That is a deliberate contrast between brand colour and action colour — worth keeping, but it means the gold has to carry the entire brand read on this screen, which is the argument for the wordmark being present here even though the nav can drop it.

What ships from this

  1. Settle the gold. #e1ad66 or #c89b5e — one of them, applied to both the artwork and --text-accent / --border-accent / --border-focus. Nothing else here is blocked, but this is.
  2. Export three assets: the tile symbol (SVG, for the favicon and the nav), the horizontal lockup (SVG, wordmark outlined), and the one-colour glyph (SVG, currentColor) for light grounds and single-ink printing. Add a fourth — the glyph on a square, unrounded gold field — if a PWA or native icon is in scope.
  3. Then the repo change is small: the symbol replaces public/vite.svg as the favicon, the Vite boilerplate <title> in index.html becomes the product name, and the symbol lands in the nav and above the auth card.

Export the mask, not a flattened path. The counter being a hole is the whole idea — it is what makes the mark inherit its ground, and it is why there is no second colourway to maintain. A Figma “copy as SVG” that outlines the strokes into one filled shape throws that away silently, and the result looks correct until the first time the mark sits on anything but the canvas colour.

Two defects in the file as it came out of Figma, for whoever does the export. The viewBox was 0 0 121 122 around a 121×116 tile, leaving six units of dead space at the bottom that silently breaks any centring downstream — the canonical box is square. And the generated mask0_119_11 / clip0_119_11 ids collide with themselves if the SVG is inlined more than once on a page; this board avoids that by defining the mask once and referencing it everywhere, and a React component would need useId.
